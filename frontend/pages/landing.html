Traffic signals that learn from the road

Most signals run on a fixed timer, whatever the traffic looks like. SmartSignal uses a Deep Q-Network (DQN) to adjust green times from live traffic conditions, so vehicles wait less and queues stay short.

Get started Login How it works

What this project does

An AI controller for a single 4-way intersection, trained and tested in simulation on real traffic volumes.

⚠️

The problem

Fixed-time signals ignore real density. On our test hours the fixed-time baseline averaged a 30.1 s wait per vehicle, with the worst single wait at 1,917 s.

🎯

The goal

Cut vehicle waiting time, shorten queues and raise throughput by choosing signal timings dynamically.

🧠

The method

A DQN agent (PyTorch) picks which phase turns green and for how long, and is rewarded for short queues, fair waits and more vehicles getting through.

🧪

The testbed

SUMO traffic simulator controlled from Python via TraCI, with 2 lanes per approach and N/S and E/W phases.

How the AI learns

The reinforcement learning loop in short.

  1. Observe: 35 values describe the intersection (queue, count, wait and occupancy for 8 lanes, plus the current phase and its elapsed time).
  2. Act: pick one of 8 actions: a phase (N/S or E/W) and a green duration. A 3 s yellow is added when switching.
  3. Get rewarded: reward = − queue cost − 0.5 × wait cost + 0.05 per vehicle through.
  4. Learn: experience replay and a target network train the 35→128→128→8 network over many simulated hours of real demand.

The dataset

Real hourly vehicle counts drive the simulated demand.

The Traffic Prediction Dataset on Kaggle contains hourly counts of vehicles at four road junctions, recorded over roughly 20 months (Nov 2015 – Jun 2017). Each row holds a timestamp, a junction number, the vehicle count and an ID. Traffic is strongly shaped by time of day and day of week, which suits studying signal control under realistic peaks and quiet periods.
